1. What evaluation metrics did you choose and why are they appropriate for your problem?
I used ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Brier score, and F1-score because this is an imbalanced binary classification 
problem where predicting defaults (the minority class) is critical. ROC-AUC evaluates overall ranking 
ability, while PR-AUC focuses more on performance for the minority class, which is more meaningful here. 
The Brier score measures how well-calibrated the predicted probabilities are, and F1-score balances precision 
and recall when choosing a classification threshold. Together, these metrics provide a comprehensive view of 
both prediction quality and probability reliability.


2. How does your model compare to the baseline?
Compared to the baseline Logistic Regression model, both XGBoost models perform better across most metrics, 
especially PR-AUC and ROC-AUC. The XGB (Full) model performs best overall, benefiting from additional features 
like grade and sub_grade and its ability to capture non-linear relationships. Logistic Regression, while more 
interpretable, shows lower predictive power but slightly more stable probability calibration. This confirms that 
more complex models improve performance at the cost of interpretability.


3. What does feature importance suggest about the patterns your model is using?
Feature importance from the XGBoost model suggests that variables related to credit risk, such as FICO score, 
interest rate, and credit history (credit_age_years), are highly influential in predicting defaults. Engineered 
features like debt-to-income ratio and revolving utilization also contribute significantly, indicating that both 
borrower financial behavior and credit capacity are key signals. Additionally, categorical features like loan purpose 
and home ownership help capture borrower profiles. Overall, the model is learning meaningful financial patterns consistent 
with real-world lending risk.
