DataExploration Part3


- Which feature or relationships do you want to explore?
    A. Credit Score (FICO) vs Default
    Why this variable?
     FICO score is the core measure of creditworthiness and is directly used in underwriting decisions. It should have a strong relationship with loan default.

    What did I expect to find?
     Lower FICO scores → higher default probability
     A roughly monotonic relationship (default decreases as FICO increases)

    B. Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI) vs Default
    Why this variable?
     DTI measures financial burden. Borrowers with high debt relative to income are more likely to experience repayment difficulties.

    What did I expect?
     Higher DTI → higher default rates
     Possibly nonlinear increase at extreme values

    C. Loan Term (36 vs 60 months)
    Why this variable?
     Longer repayment periods increase exposure to economic risk and borrower life events.

    What did I expect?
     60-month loans to have higher default rates than 36-month loans

    D. Interest Rate vs Default
    Why this variable?
     Interest rate reflects LendingClub’s internal risk assessment.

    What did I expect?
     Higher interest rates strongly associated with higher default
     Possibly strong positive correlation

    E. Annual Income vs Default
    Why this variable?
     Income reflects repayment capacity.

    What did I expect?
     Lower income → higher default
     Possibly diminishing effect after moderate income levels

    F. Grade / Sub-grade vs Default
    Why this variable?
     Institution-generated risk classification.

    What did I expect?
     Clear separation of default rates across grades
     Strong ordinal pattern (A lowest risk → G highest risk)


- What did the visualizations reveal?
    * Are relationships linear, nonlinear, weak, or strong?
    A. FICO vs Default
     Findings:
      Strong inverse relationship
      Borrowers with lower FICO ranges default significantly more
      Relationship appears nonlinear, especially below ~650

     Interpretation:
      Clear predictive power
      Default probability increases sharply at low credit scores
    
     Strength: Strong

    B. DTI vs Default
     Findings:
      Moderate positive relationship
      Higher DTI associated with higher default
      Nonlinear pattern — default rises more steeply at extreme DTI levels

     Strength: Moderate

    C. Loan Term vs Default
     Findings:
      60-month loans show noticeably higher default rates
      Clear group difference between 36 and 60 months
     
     Strength: Moderate to strong group effect

    D. Interest Rate vs Default
     Findings:
      Very strong positive relationship
      Higher interest loans default more often
      Relationship appears nearly monotonic

     Strength: Strong

        ⚠ Important:
        Interest rate is highly correlated with grade/sub-grade.

    E. Annual Income vs Default
     Findings:
      Weak to moderate inverse relationship
      Strong skew in income distribution
      Relationship weaker than expected after controlling for FICO

     Strength: Weak to moderate

    F. Grade / Sub-grade vs Default
     Findings:
      Extremely clear ordinal separation
      Default increases steadily from A → G
    
     Strength: Very strong

        Potential redundancy: Grade overlaps with interest rate and FICO.
    
    * Did you observe clusters or group differences?
     Yes — several:

     1. Clear term-based clusters
      36-month loans cluster with lower default
      60-month loans cluster with higher default

     2. Credit-score risk clusters
      Low FICO borrowers cluster at high default rates
      High FICO borrowers cluster at low default

     3. Grade-based segmentation
      Strong segmentation by risk grade
     
     These patterns suggest:
      Underwriting risk tiers are meaningful
      Model should capture nonlinear thresholds
    
    Are any variables potentially redundant?
     Yes — strong redundancy detected:

     1. fico_range_low and fico_range_high
      Nearly perfectly correlated
      Can combine into average FICO

     2. grade, sub_grade, and int_rate
      All encode similar risk information
      Including all may introduce multicollinearity

        Recommendation:
         Keep either grade OR interest rate for interpretation clarity
         Or test models with and without th

- What surprised you?
    Did any relationship contradict your initial hypothesis?
    Did any feature behave differently than expected?
     
     A. Income was less predictive than expected
     Initial hypothesis:
      Income would strongly reduce default probability

     Observed:
      After controlling for FICO and grade, income effect is weaker

     Possible explanation:
      Credit score already captures financial stability
      Income is self-reported (noise)

     B. Interest rate was extremely predictive
     This confirms:
      LendingClub pricing reflects risk accurately
      Institutional variables encode significant signal

     But raises modeling question:
      Are we predicting default independently, or just reproducing existing underwriting?

     C. Some high-income borrowers still defaulted
     This contradicts the simple assumption:
     “High income = safe borrower”

     Explanation:
      High debt burden
      Long-term loans
      Risk pricing factors


Final Analytical Reflection: 
-Credit quality variables (FICO, grade, interest rate) are strongest predictors.
-Debt burden variables (DTI, term) add meaningful signal.
-Income and employment variables provide weaker incremental value.
-Some variables are redundant and may require feature selection.
